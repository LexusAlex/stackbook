# Goal and starting point

**After this lesson, you will be able to name one task you want to learn English for and describe what you can already do and what you find difficult.** You will try reading, listening, speaking and writing, then save the results.

Allow 20–25 minutes. No previous English knowledge is required. Prepare a note and a voice recorder on your phone or computer.

These tasks help you decide where to start learning. They do not establish an A1 or A2 level: that requires a wider assessment.

## 1. Choose one practical goal

Think about a situation in which you need English. Choose one task to work towards:

- **Introductions:** give your name and city, and ask the other person's name.
- **Travel:** order a drink and ask the price.
- **Messages:** write a short message about yourself and ask one question.

Describe your goal as an action you can check. For example:

> I want to introduce myself to someone new. To check this, I will say my name and city without a script and ask the other person's name.

Write your own goal in your first language. Choose a date to return to it for the first time, such as a week from now. This is a date for comparing results; learning to do the task may take longer.

## 2. Try the four skills

First try the tasks without a translator, dictionary or prepared answers. If you cannot do something, write “I cannot do this yet”. Support comes after all four tasks.

### Reading

Read this short text:

> Hi! My name is Alex. I live in Moscow. I work in IT. I like music.

Answer in English or your first language:

1. Which city does Alex live in?
2. What field does he work in?
3. What does he like?

Save your answers. Note which words you understood immediately and where you guessed from a familiar name.

### Listening

You will hear two colleagues meeting. Listen for two facts:

1. What is the woman's name?
2. Which country is she from?

Open [British Council: Meeting a new team member](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/listening/a1/meeting-new-team-member) and play the audio on the page. Do not read **Transcript** or do the preparation tasks yet: we want to record what you can understand without help.

Listen once and write down what you heard. If needed, listen a second time and separately note what you understood after repeating. If you cannot answer, write down one familiar word or note that you cannot yet pick out words in the speech.

### Speaking

Start your voice recorder and try to talk about yourself for 20–30 seconds: give your name, city and one interest. Speak without a prepared script.

If you can only say your name, save that short result. If you do not know how to begin, note this. For a first sample, you can record an attempt to say a familiar English word.

Save the file with a date, for example `2026-10-24-english-start.m4a`. Note what made it difficult: missing words, pronouncing a sound or putting a sentence together. You do not need to publish the recording.

### Writing

Write two or three short sentences about yourself: your name, city and something you like. Use your own details. Do not copy the sentences from the reading task yet.

Save your first attempt, including mistakes. If you can only write individual words, save those. Add a note in your first language about what you wanted to say but could not yet express.

## 3. Check and try with support

Now open the answers. Compare them with your first attempt. Save corrections and anything you do with the model separately.

::: details Answers and support — open after your first attempt

**Reading:** Alex lives in Moscow, works in IT and likes music.

**Listening:** the woman's name is Carla, and she is from Brazil. Check these details in **Transcript** on the British Council page. After checking, you can listen again while reading and compare the sounds with the written words.

Use these three models for another attempt at speaking or writing about yourself. Russian translations are included for Russian-speaking learners:

| English | Russian |
| --- | --- |
| My name is Alex. | Меня зовут Алекс. |
| I live in Moscow. | Я живу в Москве. |
| I like music. | Мне нравится музыка. |

Replace the name, city and interest with your own. If you do not know a word you need, you can now look it up in a dictionary and listen to its pronunciation. Note where you needed help.

If you could not record any speech before seeing the support, read one sentence with your own details into the recorder. Save it with the note “with a model”, and mark your first attempt as “could not begin”.

For speaking, check whether you could give your details and finish your thought. For writing, is it clear what your name is, where you live and what you like? Compare your word order with the model. A teacher or conversation partner can help assess pronunciation; a recording alone is not enough to check it confidently yourself.

:::

A common starting mistake is to treat copied text as something you produced independently. Keep both versions: what you could do without help and what you managed with a model.

Describe difficulties specifically. For example: “I found the city in the text but could not recognise the country in the recording” or “I can write my name but do not know how to talk about an interest”.

## 4. Save your starting point

Copy this template into your note and fill it in:

```text
Date:
My practical goal:
How I will check the result:
Reading — what I understood independently:
Listening — what I understood the first time / after repeating:
Speaking — what I could say without a script:
Writing — what I wrote independently:
Where I needed help:
Main difficulty:
Filename of my first recording:
Date of my next check:
```

The lesson is complete when you have one goal with a clear way to check it, a result or specific difficulty for each of the four skills, your first piece of writing and a short voice recording. The number of correct answers is not a completion requirement here.

## 5. Return to the check on another day

On your chosen date, talk about yourself again and write a short message without a model. Save the new versions alongside the first ones and compare: what can you now do without help?

Use a new reading example:

> Hello! I'm Lena. I live in Kazan. I like films.

Where does Lena live, and what does she like? Answer independently first.

::: details Check the new example

Lena lives in Kazan and likes films.

:::

If you listen to the same dialogue again, note that you already know the recording. To check your understanding of unfamiliar speech, you will need a different short conversation on a similar topic.
