# Goal and starting point

**In this lesson, you will choose what you need English for, try four small tasks and save your starting results.** Later, you can compare them with a new attempt.

By the end, you will have one completed note, some written words or sentences and a short voice recording. You can complete the lesson even if you are starting from zero: each task explains what to do when you cannot answer yet.

Allow about 25–35 minutes. You can split the work into two short sessions: independent attempts first, then work with support. This lesson does not establish an A1 or A2 level.

## 1. Prepare a place for your results

1. Open an app you are comfortable writing in, such as your usual notes app or a text editor.
2. Create a note called **English — lesson 1**. Put all of today's written answers in it.
3. Find the **voice recorder** or another audio recording tool on your phone or computer. For now, just make sure you know where its record and stop buttons are.
4. Copy the template below into your note. Fill it in as you go; for now, enter only the date.

```text
Date:
My goal:
How I will check my goal:
Reading — city / work / interest:
Listening, attempt 1 — name / country:
Listening, attempt 2 — name / country:
Speaking without a model:
Writing without a model:
What I managed with support:
What was difficult:
Recording filename(s):
Date of my next check:
```

**Result of this step:** your note exists, the template is copied and the date is filled in. After each task, you will add a few words to the matching field.

## 2. Write down one clear goal

Choose one situation in which you need English:

| Situation | What you want to learn to do |
| --- | --- |
| Introductions | Give your name and city, and ask the other person's name |
| Travel | Order a drink and ask the price |
| Messages | Write two or three sentences about yourself and ask a question |

If choosing is difficult, use **introductions**. Fill in two lines in your first language. For example:

```text
My goal: meet someone using English.
How I will check my goal: without a prepared script, give my name
and city and ask the other person's name.
```

Use this goal or write your own following the same pattern: **situation → specific action → how to check**. At this stage, writing the goal in your first language is enough; you do not have to perform it in English yet.

**Result of this step:** you have one goal and a clear way to check it. Choose a date for a first repeat check, such as a week from now.

## 3. Try four skills without hints

“Without hints” means trying first without opening the answers, translator or dictionary. We want to keep that first attempt. If you do not know an answer, write **I do not know yet** and continue. You will be able to use models after these four tasks.

### Reading

Here you are trying to understand written text. You do not need to read it aloud.

1. Read this short text once or twice:

   > Hi! My name is Alex. I live in Moscow. I work in IT. I like music.

2. Find three details: which city Alex lives in, what field he works in and what he likes.
3. Put your answers under **Reading — city / work / interest**. You can use individual words in your first language.
4. If you find only one detail, write it down and put “I do not know yet” for the others.

For example, the form of an answer could be: `city: ...; work: I do not know yet; interest: ...`. Keep the correct answers closed for now.

**What to save:** three answers or notes about difficulties. This completes the reading task.

### Listening

Listening means understanding spoken language. You will hear two colleagues talking. You only need to find two facts: **the woman's name and which country she is from**.

1. Find the player below and press **▶**. The recording is about a minute and a half long.
2. Listen once without reading the conversation. Do not try to translate every turn.
3. Under **Listening, attempt 1**, write the name and country you heard. If unsure how to spell the name, write what you think you heard.
4. Move the recording back to the start and press **▶** again. Fill in **Listening, attempt 2**. Keep the first line as it was.
5. If you cannot hear either fact, write “name: did not understand; country: did not understand”. That is still a result for your first check.

### Speaking

Now try to say something in English without a written script.

1. Open your voice recorder and press record.
2. Try to give your name, city and one interest. Say whatever you can; aim for up to 20–30 seconds.
3. Press stop. Listen to your recording once.
4. Save it with a clear name, such as `lesson-01-first-attempt`. You can add a date to the filename. You do not need to change its extension.
5. Under **Speaking without a model**, note what you managed: for example, “said only my name” or “said my name and city but could not give an interest”.

**If you do not know any phrases:** write “I cannot introduce myself yet” and continue to writing. After the explanation, you will record one phrase using a model. That is enough to complete this lesson if you note the support you used.

**What to save:** a voice recording of your independent attempt, or a specific note that you could not begin yet.

### Writing

1. Return to your note. Under **Writing without a model**, try to write two or three English sentences: your name, where you live and something you like.
2. Use only what you remember for now. Do not copy the reading text.
3. Keep what you wrote, including mistakes. Do not replace it with a corrected version.
4. If you can only write your name or an individual word, keep those. If you cannot write a sentence at all, add a note in your first language: “I want to write that ...; I do not know how yet”.

**What to save:** your first writing attempt. Even a few words can show your starting point.

## 4. Open the answers and try with support

The independent part is finished. You can now open the block below, read translations, listen with the text and use a dictionary.

::: details Answers and support — open after the four tasks

**Reading answers:** city — Moscow; work — IT; interest — music.

The full Russian translation is: «Привет! Меня зовут Алекс. Я живу в Москве. Я работаю в IT. Мне нравится музыка».

**Listening answers:** the woman's name is Carla, and she is from Brazil. On the British Council page, open **Transcript** and find her name and answer about her country. Listen to those turns again while looking at the text.

Use these three models for your own introduction:

| Phrase | Russian meaning | What to replace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| My name is Alex. | Меня зовут Алекс. | Alex → your name |
| I live in Moscow. | Я живу в Москве. | Moscow → your city |
| I like music. | Мне нравится музыка. | music → your interest |

Start with one phrase about your name. Then add your city and interest if you can. For example, for someone called Anna from Kazan who likes music:

> My name is Anna. I live in Kazan. I like music.

You will hear *my name's* in the recording: it is a short form of *my name is*. For your first recording, you can repeat the beginning of the speaker's first turn and substitute your own name.

:::

Now do four things:

1. Compare your reading and listening answers with your first attempt. Add “correct”, “corrected” or “did not understand without text” alongside each.
2. Under **What I managed with support**, write one to three sentences about yourself using the table. Replace the example's details with your own. You can now look up an unknown word and listen to its dictionary pronunciation.
3. Read your sentences aloud, then record them. Save a separate recording called `lesson-01-with-model`. This is a recording **with a model**; it does not replace the first attempt.
4. Put the filename in your note. If you made no independent recording, this supported attempt will be your first saved recording, labelled accordingly.

**How to check speaking and writing:** do they contain your own name, city and interest? Have you kept the model's word order? Where did you need to look at the answer? You do not have to grade your pronunciation if you cannot assess it yet: note a specific difficulty to revisit with a teacher or conversation partner.

## 5. Finish your note and today's lesson

Under **What was difficult**, write one or two specific difficulties. For example: “I understand the city name in the text but cannot recognise the country in the audio” or “I can repeat the name phrase but forget the words without a model”.

Here is a **completed note from an imaginary learner**. These are examples of how to record results, not results you are required to achieve.

```text
Date: 24 October 2026
My goal: meet someone using English.
How I will check my goal: give my name and city, ask their name.
Reading — city / work / interest: Moscow / IT / music.
Listening, attempt 1 — name / country: did not catch either.
Listening, attempt 2 — name / country: heard Carla / did not catch country.
Speaking without a model: said only my name.
Writing without a model: wrote my name, could not make a sentence.
What I managed with support: wrote and read three sentences about myself.
What was difficult: cannot recall the phrase about my city independently.
Recording filename(s): lesson-01-first-attempt; lesson-01-with-model.
Date of my next check: 31 October 2026.
```

**Check before finishing:**

- You have one goal and a way to check it.
- Each of the four tasks has an answer or a specific note about a difficulty.
- Your first writing attempt is kept separately from corrections.
- You have a short voice recording labelled to show whether you used a model.
- You have recorded difficulties and a date for your next check.

When these points are complete, you can move on to [Reviewing words and phrases](./reviewing-words-and-phrases). **You do not need to wait for the repeat-check date.** Today's results provide a comparison, so mistakes and “I do not know yet” answers do not prevent you from finishing.

## 6. What to do on your repeat-check date

1. Without rereading the old model, try again to say and write a few sentences about yourself.
2. Save the new versions alongside the first ones, for example as `lesson-01-recheck`.
3. Compare: what can you now do independently, and where do you still need support?
4. Try a new reading text:

   > Hello! I'm Lena. I live in Kazan. I like films.

   Write where Lena lives and what she likes. Only then open the answer.

::: details Check the new example

Lena lives in Kazan and likes films.

:::

If you listen to the same dialogue again, note that you already know it. Understanding a familiar recording and understanding a new conversation are checked separately.
