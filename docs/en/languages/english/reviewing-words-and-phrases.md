# Reviewing words and phrases

**In this lesson, you will make five introduction cards, practise recalling phrases before revealing the answer and record dates for later reviews.**

Today's task is to prepare the set and do your first check. Reviews on later days will become a short part of future sessions; you do not have to finish two weeks of reviews before learning more.

Allow about 25–35 minutes for the first pass and 5–10 minutes per review. If copying the cards is difficult, split the preparation into two short sessions.

## 1. Prepare your note and five cards

Open your note from [Goal and starting point](./goal-and-starting-point). Without looking at the model, try to give your name in English. If you cannot, note it: that phrase will go into today's review set.

For your first set, **five small pieces of paper** are a convenient option. Put a task in your first language on one side and the answer and example on the other. To try it on screen first, use the five expandable blocks in step 3: a closed block shows the task, and an open one shows the answer.

In your note, create a section called **English — lesson 2** and copy this template:

```text
Date I created the set:
Where I keep my five cards:

Card 1, greeting — first-attempt result / next date:
Card 2, my name — first-attempt result / next date:
Card 3, asking a name — first-attempt result / next date:
Card 4, pleased to meet — first-attempt result / next date:
Card 5, where someone is from — first-attempt result / next date:

What was difficult:
My phrases without an open model:
```

**Result of this step:** you know where to find the cards and where to record results. If using the blocks on this page, save the lesson link under “Where I keep my five cards”.

3. After one attempt, open “Check the phrases you heard”.
4. Compare what you heard with the answers. Play the beginning again, pause after a relevant turn and say the phrase after the speaker.

## 3. Make your five-card set

The **front** is the side you look at before answering. It contains only the task. Reveal the **back** after your attempt: it contains the English phrase, translation, example and pronunciation source.

For example, a name card could look like this:

```text
FRONT
Give your name.

BACK
Phrase: My name's Alex.
Russian translation: Меня зовут Алекс.
Example: Hi! My name's Alex.
Audio: Meeting a new team member, Peter's first turn.
```

Substitute your own name. Keep the dialogue link in your note; on paper, the title and a cue about the turn are enough. [British Council](https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/professional-development/teachers/teaching-knowledge-database/t-w/vocabulary-bank) describes the phrase, translation and example format for personal vocabulary collections.

**Do the following for each of the five cards below:**

1. Read the task in the heading.
2. Click the heading to reveal the English phrase and translation. Russian translations are provided for Russian-speaking learners.
3. Find it in the audio using the cue about the relevant turn, listen and say it aloud twice. One dialogue provides the audio for the whole set.
4. If making paper cards, copy the task onto the front and the phrase, translation, example and audio cue onto the back. Keep the English answer off the front.
5. Close the block or turn the card task-side up. Do not assess your memory yet: you are learning the material at this stage.

::: details 1. Greet someone

**Phrase:** Hi! — «Привет!»

**Example:** Hi, Alex! — «Привет, Алекс!»

**Audio:** [the British Council conversation](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/listening/a1/meeting-new-team-member), the greeting in Peter's first turn.

:::

::: details 2. Give your name

**Phrase:** My name's Peter. — «Меня зовут Питер».

**Personal example:** My name's Alex. — «Меня зовут Алекс». Replace Alex with your own name on your card.

**Audio:** [the British Council conversation](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/listening/a1/meeting-new-team-member), Peter's first turn. *My name's* is a short form of *My name is* from lesson 1.

:::

::: details 3. Ask someone's name

**Phrase:** What's your name? — «Как вас зовут?»

**Example:** What's your name? — My name's Alex. — «Как вас зовут?» — «Меня зовут Алекс».

**Audio:** [the British Council conversation](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/listening/a1/meeting-new-team-member), the question in Peter's first turn.

:::

::: details 4. Say you are pleased to meet

**Phrase:** It's nice to meet you. — «Приятно познакомиться».

**Example:** It's nice to meet you, Anna. — «Приятно познакомиться, Анна».

**Audio:** [the British Council conversation](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/listening/a1/meeting-new-team-member), the turns about meeting near the beginning.

:::

::: details 5. Ask where someone is from

**Phrase:** Where are you from? — «Откуда вы?»

**Example:** Where are you from, Alex? — «Откуда вы, Алекс?»

**Audio:** [the British Council conversation](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/listening/a1/meeting-new-team-member), Peter's question about Carla's country near the end.

:::

**Result of this step:** you have five paper cards or a saved link to the set above. You can show yourself a task while keeping the English answer hidden.

## 4. Complete one whole check

Hide all the answers. Shuffle paper cards. On screen, choose blocks in a different order, such as **3 → 1 → 5 → 2 → 4**.

For one card, do this:

1. Look only at the task, such as “Give your name”.
2. Try to say the phrase in English. Give yourself about five seconds. Keep the answer closed until you have tried.
3. Turn the card over or open the block. Compare your phrase with the model: did you say all the necessary words in the right order?
4. Record the result of your **first attempt** in your note, using the table below.
5. If you made an error or looked at the answer, listen and repeat it correctly. Then hide it and take two other cards. Return to the difficult card after those and try again.

| What happened before you revealed the answer | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| You said the required phrase independently | independent |
| You tried but missed or mixed up words | with an error |
| You could not answer, or looked at the model first | with a hint |

**A complete worked example:** you see “Give your name”, cannot recall the phrase, reveal the answer and read it. Write “card 2 — with a hint”. Repeat the phrase, hide it, check two other cards and try giving your name again. If you can now do it, add “recalled after correction”, keeping the record of the first attempt.

Check all five cards this way. Put the set aside for about five minutes, take a break and then check it once more.

Note pronunciation separately, for example “recalled the words; still comparing the sound with the audio”. If you cannot assess pronunciation yet, record whether you remembered the expression and listen to the model before your practice repetition.

Attempting recall, checking the answer and returning to material on different days can support retention; these principles are discussed in [AERO's guide to spacing and retrieval practice](https://www.edresearch.edu.au/guides-resources/practice-guides/spacing-and-retrieval-practice-guide-full-publication).

**Result of this step:** each card has a first-attempt result. The number of independent answers shows what still needs practice; you do not have to get all five right immediately.

## 5. Write down actual review dates

**Day 0 is the day you create the set. All day numbers in the table count from that date.** Start with this schedule for cards you can recall independently:

| Day number | Action | Example if you start on 24 October 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 0 | Prepare the set and do the first check | 24 October |
| Day 1 | Check with the answers hidden | 25 October |
| Day 3 | Review after a two-day gap | 27 October |
| Day 7 | Review after a four-day gap | 31 October |
| Day 14 | Review after a one-week gap and set the next date | 7 November |

Write your own dates in the note. This is a starting schedule for the lesson; each card's dates change with its results.

**If you forget a card:** study the answer now and schedule a check for tomorrow. For example, if you forget the name phrase on 25 October, give it a 26 October review date. Other cards you recalled independently can stay on 27 October. After successful answers, gradually increase the gap again.

If you miss a date, check the set when you next study and assign new dates based on what you remember now.

Example log **after checking on 25 October**:

| Card | First attempt | Next check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Greeting | independent | 27 October |
| My name | with a hint | 26 October |
| Asking a name | independent | 27 October |
| Pleased to meet | with an error | 26 October |
| Where someone is from | independent | 27 October |

**Result of this step:** every card in your note has a next date. Next time, start with the cards whose review date has arrived.

## 6. Say and write phrases about yourself

Cards help you recall expressions; now try using them together.

1. Close all the English answers.
2. Imagine meeting Anna. Greet her, give **your own** name and say you are pleased to meet.
3. Say these three lines aloud. If studying alone, you can speak to an imaginary conversation partner.
4. Under **My phrases without an open model**, write what you managed to say.
5. Open the model below and compare. If you needed support, note which phrase you forgot.

Also check your review method: how should you label a result if you looked at the answer first and then said the phrase?

::: details Model and check

One possible version for a learner called Alex:

> Hi, Anna! My name's Alex. It's nice to meet you.

Use your own name. The message performs three actions: greeting, introducing yourself and saying you are pleased to meet. If you managed only one or two, keep those and review the missing card.

An answer produced after revealing the model is labelled **with a hint**. If you tried without the answer first, keep that first attempt too.

:::

## 7. Check what you have done today

Today's work is finished when:

- you have a five-card set with phrases, translations, examples and an audio reference;
- you have tried answering each card before revealing the English text;
- you have recorded results and difficulties;
- every card has a next review date;
- you have tried saying and writing a short introduction using your own name.

On later days, reviewing this set will take a few minutes. **You can study further topics while continuing to review old cards.**

To keep the check useful, put only the task on the front, vary the order and compare pronunciation with the audio. Russian-letter spellings do not replace listening to the model.
