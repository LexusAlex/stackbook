# The English alphabet

**After this lesson, you will be able to find the letters in your own name in the English alphabet and say their names in English.** You will listen to letter names, match capital and small forms and make a short voice recording.

Allow about 25–35 minutes. The alphabet has 26 letters, so work in small sections. Prioritise the letters in your own name; you can practise the rest over several short sessions.

Before starting, review two or three cards from [Reviewing words and phrases](./reviewing-words-and-phrases) whose review date has arrived. Then move on to the new material.

## 1. Prepare your note and your name

1. Open your study note and add a section called **English — lesson 3**.
2. Write your name using English letters in the form you normally use. For example, Анна → `Anna`, Нина → `Nina`. If you do not know the spelling yet, start with the practice name `Anna`, then check your own name's spelling before the final task.
3. Prepare your voice recorder, as in lesson 1.
4. Copy the template. Fill in the date and name now; complete the rest as you work.

```text
Date:
My name using English letters:
The individual letters in my name:
Uppercase and lowercase letters I mixed up:
Listening — first four letters, attempt 1:
Listening — attempt 2, if needed:
How I said my name's letters without the table:
What I still need to review:
Recording filename:
Date of my next check:
```

**Result of this step:** your name is written down, and you have a place for your answers and recording reference.

## 2. Listen to the letter names

The English alphabet has **26 letters**. Each has an uppercase, or capital, form and a lowercase, or small, form: for example, `A` and `a` are the same letter.

When you say the individual letters in a name, you use **letter names**. They do not specify one unchanging sound for every word: reading rules will be a separate topic. Today's task is to hear a letter's name and recognise the matching letter.

1. Press **▶** and listen once to the whole alphabet. The recording lasts about 31 seconds.
2. Follow the table below with your eyes as you listen: A, B, C and onwards in order.
3. Play the recording again. Pause after a few letters and repeat them aloud.
4. Work in sections: **A–G**, then **H–N**, **O–T**, **U–Z**. If you get tired, leave some letters for your next short session.

## 3. Recognise different forms of letters

Some English letters resemble Russian ones, but their names differ. For example, say the English `B` as it sounds in the recording. When typing, switch your keyboard layout to English.

1. Look at these six small letters: **b, d, g, h, r, y**.
2. Write the matching capital letter for each, in the same order. Try without the table first.
3. Then write **Anna** entirely in capital letters. Keep every letter, including repeated ones.
4. Only after your attempt, open the check.

::: details Check uppercase and lowercase letters

- `b, d, g, h, r, y` → **B, D, G, H, R, Y**.
- `Anna` → **ANNA**: four letters, with N appearing twice.

If you mixed up a pair, find it in the table and write both forms together, such as `G — g`. Then close the table and try that pair again.

:::

**What to save:** your answer and the specific pairs you mixed up. If everything was correct, write “matched letter forms without an error”.

## 4. Write letters from listening

The next recording reads letters **in a different order**, in groups by sound. For now, you only need the first group: **four letters**. The answer is hidden below.

1. Close the answers and keep the letter table out of view.
2. Press **▶** in the second player and write the first four names as letters: `1. ... 2. ... 3. ... 4. ...`.
3. Pause after the fourth letter. A different group follows.
4. If needed, return to the beginning and listen again. Record your second attempt separately.
5. Open the answer and compare each position.

The first group is **A — H — J — K**.

If you wrote `A — H — G — K`, for example, note that you mixed up G and J. Find both letters in the first recording: G is /dʒiː/, and J is /dʒeɪ/. Listen and repeat them separately, then return to the task.

If you could not recognise any letters yet, open the answer, listen to the first four while reading and repeat. Label that result “with the answer”, then try again without it later.

:::

**What to save:** your first attempt, your repeat result and the letters you needed support to identify.

## 5. Say the letters in your own name

Now apply the alphabet to your own task.

1. Return to the name from step 1. Under it, write each letter separately: for example, `Alex` → **A — L — E — X**.
2. Find those letters in the table and the first recording. Listen to their names and say each one aloud.
3. Say the whole sequence slowly, with a small pause between letters. For `Alex`, that is A, then L, then E, then X. Use the English letter names from the audio.
4. Close the table, look only at the written name and say its letters again.
5. Start your voice recorder. First say the familiar phrase **My name's ...**, using your own name. Then say the name's letters in order.
6. Save the recording as `lesson-03-alphabet` and put the filename in your note.

Another example: `Anna` → **A — N — N — A**. Say the two Ns separately: for today, simply repeat the letter name twice.

**How to check:** did you say every letter, keep repeated letters and preserve the order? If working with a partner, ask them to write the letters they hear and compare with your name. If studying alone, listen to your recording while checking the sequence against the written name. Use the audio or a teacher's help for pronunciation you are unsure about.

If you can only do it with the table, record “with the table” and practise the necessary letters again. Then make a separate attempt without support.

## 6. Complete your note and schedule a review

An example note from **an imaginary learner called Alex**:

```text
Date: 25 October 2026
My name using English letters: Alex.
The individual letters in my name: A — L — E — X.
Uppercase and lowercase letters I mixed up: initially G and g; corrected.
Listening — first four letters, attempt 1: A — H — G — K.
Listening — attempt 2, if needed: A — H — J — K.
How I said my name's letters without the table: managed after two reviews.
What I still need to review: E and I; G and J.
Recording filename: lesson-03-alphabet.
Date of my next check: 26 October 2026.
```

Today's work is complete when you have:

- matched uppercase and lowercase letters and checked your answers;
- tried to write four letters from listening, kept the result and corrected mistakes;
- written your name as individual letters and tried to say them without the table;
- saved a short voice recording and noted where you still needed support;
- chosen a date for your next short review, such as tomorrow.

At your next check, first say the letters of your own name without the table. For letters you forget, add one card per letter following the previous lesson: the letter on the front, its name and a recording link on the back.

For a new example, try saying the letters in **Mia**, then open the check.

::: details Check the new example

`Mia` → **M — I — A**. Letter names: /em/ — /aɪ/ — /eɪ/.

:::

Continue practising the remaining letters in short sections. Confidently naming the letters in your own name is your first practical result; you can gradually work towards saying the entire alphabet fluently.
