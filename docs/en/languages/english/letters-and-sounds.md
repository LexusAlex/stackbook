# Letters and sounds

**After this lesson, you will be able to open a dictionary entry, find its transcription, listen to the recording and check how to pronounce a new word.** You will work through five short words and independently check one more.

Allow about 25–35 minutes. If needed, split the work into two sessions: examples and dictionary practice first, then the independent check.

Before starting, say the letters in your own name from [The English alphabet](./alphabet). Today you will add a different skill to naming letters: pronouncing a whole word. Russian translations below support Russian-speaking learners.

## 2. Separate letters, letter names and word pronunciation

Look at this example:

| What you see | What it represents | What to do with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `cat` | The spelling of the word for a cat | Recognise or write the word |
| `C — A — T` | The word's individual letters | Say their alphabet names: /siː/ — /eɪ/ — /tiː/ |
| `/kæt/` | A written representation of the word's pronunciation | Listen and say the whole word |

We see and write **letters**. We produce and hear **sounds**. When saying the word cat, we say /kæt/. Listing the names C, A and T is useful when giving its spelling, but it does not replace pronouncing the word itself.

Compare two words. First press **▶** for cat, listen and repeat. Then do the same for cake.

## 3. Find a dictionary transcription and audio

A **transcription** represents pronunciation using special symbols. In Cambridge Dictionary it usually appears between slashes, such as `/kæt/`. The symbols may resemble English letters, but they represent pronunciation.

For now, learn where to find the transcription and compare it with the audio. You do not need to memorise all the symbols at once. For an unfamiliar symbol, use [Cambridge Dictionary's pronunciation-symbol help with audio](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/help/phonetics.html).

Complete one whole dictionary check with **cat**:

1. Open [the dictionary entry for cat](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/cat). Find the word **cat** near the beginning of the entry.
2. Look for **UK** and **US** beside the headword. They identify British and American pronunciations. Choose **UK** for today's work.
3. Find **/kæt/** next to UK and copy it into the “Transcription” field. The slashes mark the boundaries of the transcription; they are not pronounced themselves.
4. Click the speaker icon beside **UK**. Listen first, then click again and repeat after the speaker.
5. Hide or look away from the transcription, look only at **cat** and say the whole word. Then listen to the model once more.
6. Fill in the word, meaning, UK variety, transcription and entry link in your note.

For example, you could record this part as:

```text
Word: cat.
Meaning: кошка.
Variety: UK.
Transcription: /kæt/.
Source: Cambridge Dictionary, cat entry.
Observation: I hear /æ/ in the word, rather than the name of A.
```

If you cannot play the sound on the dictionary page, use the cat player above. Still try to find the transcription in the entry itself. The word links in this lesson also provide audio attribution and an alternative place to listen.

**Result of this step:** you have found a dictionary transcription, chosen a pronunciation variety and played its recording.

Notice how spelling relates to pronunciation in these particular examples:

- In **book**, the two letters **oo** together represent /ʊ/. You do not say the name of O twice when pronouncing the word.
- In **phone**, **ph** represents /f/, and the final **e** is not pronounced separately.
- In **name**, **a** corresponds to /eɪ/, and the final **e** is not pronounced separately.

These observations apply to the words studied here. Check the recording again for a new word: similar spelling does not guarantee the same pronunciation.

**What to save:** choose two words and record what surprised you about their pronunciation. You can use short phrases as examples: **my book — моя книга**, **my phone — мой телефон**, **my name — моё имя**.

## 5. Check what you can distinguish

Close the explanation and answer three questions in writing:

1. Does **/fəʊn/** show the word's spelling, its letter names or its pronunciation?
2. Which two letters in **phone** together represent /f/?
3. Is **a** pronounced the same in **cat** and **cake**? If not, what does the dictionary show?

::: details Check the three answers

1. **/fəʊn/** is a transcription: a written representation of phone's pronunciation.
2. **ph** represents /f/ in this word.
3. They differ: **cat /kæt/** and **cake /keɪk/**. The first word has /æ/, the second /eɪ/.

If you made an error, return to the relevant example, listen and add a correction alongside your first attempt.

:::

## 7. Record your voice and finish your note

1. Listen to book and bag once more.
2. Hide the transcriptions. Record yourself saying **book**, then **bag**, with a pause between them. Say whole words rather than listing their letter names.
3. Save the file as **lesson-04-word-pronunciation**. Listen to it, then separately play the dictionary models.
4. Write one observation: what you managed to repeat and which sound you are still unsure about. If assessing the sound yourself is difficult, keep the recording for review with a teacher or conversation partner.
