# Word stress

**About 5 minutes: listen to three words, find the stressed syllable and say the words yourself.** The audio, exercises and checks are here. If time runs out, continue from the remaining step next time.

## 1. Compare two words — one minute

We give a **stressed syllable** more emphasis. A syllable is a part you can identify when pronouncing a word. Table has two such parts; banana has three. Listen for the part that stands out.

Press **▶** for each word and repeat aloud.

**table — стол: TA-ble.** Stress is on the first syllable.

Capitals highlight the stressed syllable; hyphens help you see the parts. These are study cues; the normal spellings are **table**, **hotel**. Count syllables by pronunciation, rather than by the number of letters.

## 2. Find the stress mark — one minute

In a dictionary transcription, **ˈ** comes **before the stressed syllable**. UK means British pronunciation.

| Word | UK transcription | Stress position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| table | /ˈteɪ.bəl/ | Before the first syllable: **TA**-ble |
| hotel | /həʊˈtel/ | Before the second syllable: ho-**TEL** |

A dot in a transcription can separate syllables; **ˈ** shows primary stress. If the other symbols are unfamiliar, find **ˈ** and follow the audio. This mark is not added to the normal spelling.

## 4. Say and check — two minutes

Look only at the normal spellings: **table → hotel → banana**. Without looking back at the cues, say all three words aloud, emphasising the stressed syllable.
